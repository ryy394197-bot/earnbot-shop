**How to Explain 1.5% Platform Commission to Supply and Demand Sides**

In the world of online marketplaces, whether you're connecting freelancers with clients, developers with projects, or service providers with customers, one of the most delicate conversations you’ll have is explaining the platform’s commission structure. A 1.5% fee may seem small, but it can be a point of contention for both sides of the transaction. The key is to communicate this clearly and transparently—without overcomplicating it.

Whether you're running a marketplace for software development, design, or consulting, the way you frame your commission model can significantly impact trust and long-term engagement. Here are some practical steps to help you explain a 1.5% platform fee in a clear and compelling way.

---

### 1. Start with Transparency from the Beginning

The first and most important rule is to be upfront about the fee from the very beginning. Whether you're onboarding a new user, writing an onboarding email, or setting up your marketplace’s terms of service, make sure the 1.5% commission is clearly stated.

For example, in your onboarding message, you could say:

> "As part of our service, we charge a 1.5% commission on every successful transaction. This helps us maintain a high-quality platform and ensure a smooth experience for all users."

By being transparent early on, you reduce the risk of surprises later and build trust with both supply and demand sides.

---

### 2. Frame the Fee as a Value-Added Cost

A 1.5% fee may seem like a cost, but it's important to frame it as a value-added investment. Explain how the platform is helping both sides by providing tools, security, dispute resolution, and visibility.

You can say something like:

> "The 1.5% commission is our way of investing in the platform’s infrastructure—ensuring that both you and your clients get a safe, reliable, and efficient experience. This includes features like secure payments, dispute resolution, and access to a growing network of professionals."

This helps both parties see the fee not as a burden, but as a cost of doing business that comes with tangible benefits.

---

### 3. Offer a Clear Example

People often respond better to concrete examples. Provide a simple calculation that shows how the fee works in practice. For instance:

> "If a client pays $1,000 for a project, the platform takes a 1.5% commission, which is $15. The freelancer receives $985, and the client gets a seamless experience with guaranteed quality and support."

This makes the fee more tangible and easier to understand, especially for those who may not be familiar with commission-based models.

---

### 4. Avoid Common Mistakes

When explaining the fee, avoid the following common pitfalls:

- **Not being specific**: Vague statements like "we charge a small fee" can confuse users. Always be specific about the percentage and what it covers.
- **Hiding the fee**: Don’t surprise users with the fee at the last moment. Make it clear from the start.
- **Overemphasizing the cost**: While it's important to be honest, don’t make the fee sound like a major obstacle. Focus on the value the platform brings.
- **Ignoring the perspective of both sides**: Make sure to explain the fee in a way that is fair and reasonable to both the service provider and the client.

---

### 5. Reinforce Trust with Clear Terms

Finally, make sure that the terms of the commission are clearly outlined in your marketplace’s policies. This includes:

- When the fee is charged (e.g., upon successful transaction).
- What the fee covers (e.g., payment processing, platform maintenance, support).
- Whether the fee is refundable or not in case of disputes.

By having clear and accessible terms, you reduce confusion and build a foundation of trust with your users.
