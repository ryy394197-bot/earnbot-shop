Title: How to Charge for SUPER and Ti Postfixes Separately? A Practical Guide

---

## Introduction

In the realm of web hosting and domain management, many service providers have adopted a strategy where they offer two types of packages with different levels of support or features. For instance, some companies may provide "SUPER" plans that come with extra benefits like 24/7 customer support or advanced security measures, while others might offer "Ti" plans that are slightly more affordable but still deliver solid value.

However, these SUPER and Ti postfixes often end up being bundled together in a single price point. This can create confusion for customers who may want to access the premium features of the SUPER plan without paying for everything included in the Ti package. Moreover, it might lead to potential upsell scenarios where customers are encouraged to buy the SUPER plan even if they only need the basic Ti features.

This article aims to provide a practical guide on how to effectively charge for these separate postfixes. By doing so, you can ensure fair pricing and maximize your revenue by offering clear value propositions without sacrificing customer satisfaction or trust.

## Actionable Tips

### 1. **Clearly Define Each Postfix’s Features**

Before implementing any charging strategy, it's essential to clearly define the features that come with each postfix. This ensures transparency for both your customers and internal teams responsible for managing pricing and support requests.

- **SUPER:** Highlight all premium benefits such as 24/7 customer support, advanced security measures, dedicated account managers, etc.
- **Ti:** Emphasize basic but essential services like 99.9% uptime guarantee, automatic backups, domain monitoring, etc.

### 2. **Use Different Pricing Models**

Different postfixes should be priced based on their unique features and value propositions. This approach allows you to charge more for the SUPER plan while still offering a competitive Ti package that appeals to budget-conscious customers.

- **SUPER:** Higher price point reflecting premium benefits.
- **Ti:** Lower price point but still covering core needs with additional upsell opportunities.

### 3. **Offer Upsells and Cross-Selling Opportunities**

By clearly outlining what each postfix includes, you can offer targeted upsells or cross-sell complementary services that customers might not be aware of initially.

- For instance, if a customer opts for the Ti plan but later discovers they need dedicated email support, they can easily upgrade to the SUPER plan without losing their existing domain.
- Similarly, offering add-ons like SSL certificates, CDN services, or specialized hosting plans can help drive additional revenue.

### 4. **Educate Your Audience**

Ensure that your customers understand the value proposition behind each postfix and why it's worth paying for. This education will help build trust and justify higher prices.

- Regularly share blog posts or tutorials highlighting the benefits of choosing a specific postfix over another.
- Host webinars, workshops, or live Q&A sessions to address common concerns and answer questions about your pricing strategy.

### 5. **Adapt Your Marketing Messages**

Tailor your marketing messages to highlight the unique selling points of each postfix. Use clear, concise language that resonates with your target audience's needs and preferences.

- For example, if you're targeting developers who need advanced security features, emphasize Ti's built-in firewall and SUPER’s dedicated account manager.
- If your focus is on small businesses, promote Ti’s automatic backups and domain monitoring as key differentiators from the SUPER plan.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. **Not Clearly Differentiating Features**

Customers expect clear differentiation between packages. If both plans seem similar or offer overlapping features, they may feel misled into paying more than necessary for what they perceive as basic services.

- Example: Both plans claim "99.9% uptime," but the SUPER plan offers dedicated support and advanced security measures that aren't mentioned in the Ti description.
- Solution: Clearly differentiate between the two plans by outlining specific features and benefits.

### 2. **Overpricing or Underpricing**

Mispricing can lead to customer dissatisfaction, loss of trust, and decreased revenue. Make sure your prices accurately reflect the value provided.

- Example: A Ti plan with only basic monitoring tools is priced similarly to a SUPER plan that includes advanced analytics.
- Solution: Conduct thorough market research to ensure you're offering competitive yet fair pricing for each postfix.

### 3. **Lack of Upsell Opportunities**

If customers can't easily access additional services or features, they may feel restricted and less likely to upgrade when needed.

- Example: A customer finds out about dedicated email support but is unable to purchase it separately.
- Solution: Clearly communicate how customers can extend their Ti plan with add-ons like email hosting or specialized domains.

### 4. **Poor Communication**

Inconsistent messaging across different platforms (website, email, social media) can confuse customers and undermine your pricing strategy.

- Example: A customer receives conflicting information about the included features in an email versus on the website.
