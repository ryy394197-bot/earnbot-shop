**How Suppliers Can Set Release % Without Affecting Their Daytime Usage**

In the world of cloud computing and decentralized resource sharing, balancing the need to monetize your unused capacity with the necessity of keeping your own usage unaffected is a delicate act. As a supplier on platforms like ComputeGate, you have the opportunity to generate income by releasing a percentage of your compute resources for others to use. However, doing so without disrupting your own workflow requires a strategic approach.

### 1. Understand the Resource Allocation Model

Before setting your release percentage, it’s crucial to fully understand how the platform you're on manages resource allocation. Most platforms allow you to define the percentage of your compute power that will be available for others. However, the key is to ensure that your personal usage is prioritized in the system's scheduling logic.

For example, some platforms allow you to set a "reserved" portion of your resources that will never be released, while the remaining percentage is available for others. This ensures that your own tasks are always given priority, even when your release percentage is active.

### 2. Schedule Your Releases During Off-Peak Hours

One of the most effective strategies is to release your resources during off-peak hours. This not only minimizes the impact on your own usage but also increases the likelihood of your resources being utilized by others who may be running tasks during those times.

Many platforms provide insights into usage patterns, allowing you to determine the best times for releasing your resources. By aligning your release schedule with these patterns, you can maximize your earnings without compromising your productivity.

### 3. Use Automated Tools to Monitor and Adjust

To maintain the balance between monetization and usability, it's wise to use automated tools that can monitor your resource usage and adjust your release settings accordingly. Some platforms offer built-in dashboards or APIs that allow you to set thresholds for when and how much of your resources should be released.

For instance, you can set a rule that says, “If my CPU usage is below 30% for more than two hours, release 20% of my compute power.” This way, your resources are only made available when they are not needed for your own tasks, ensuring minimal disruption.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Releasing too much at once**: Setting a high release percentage without considering your own usage can lead to performance issues or even downtime for your own tasks.
- **Ignoring usage patterns**: Failing to analyze when your resources are underutilized can result in missed opportunities for monetization.
- **Not monitoring regularly**: Once you've set your release parameters, it's easy to forget about them. Regular monitoring and adjustments are essential for long-term success.
