**How Suppliers Can Set Release Percent Without Affecting Their Own Usage During the Day**

As a supplier in the decentralized computing or cloud resource market, one of the most critical decisions you make is how much of your computing power you're willing to release for others to use. However, many suppliers are worried that opening up their resources might interfere with their own usage during peak hours — especially during the day. The good news is, there are practical ways to set a release percentage that keeps your resources available to others while still ensuring your own work isn't disrupted.

---

### 1. **Understand Your Usage Patterns**

Before you even think about setting a release percentage, take a step back and analyze your own usage patterns. What hours of the day do you use your resources the most? Do you run batch jobs or tasks that only occur during specific times? By mapping out your usage, you can determine which hours are "safe" for releasing your resources to others.

For example, if you're a developer who primarily works from 9 AM to 5 PM, you may find that releasing your resources during the night or early morning hours is less disruptive. This allows you to take advantage of the demand from other users without sacrificing your own productivity.

---

### 2. **Set a Smart Release Schedule**

Once you've identified your non-peak hours, the next step is to set a release schedule that works for both you and your potential users. A common approach is to release a certain percentage of your resources during off-peak hours, such as between 10 PM and 4 AM.

You can use tools or platforms like **ComputeGate** to set up a dynamic release schedule. These platforms often allow you to define time-based rules, such as:

- "Release 30% of resources from 10 PM to 4 AM."
- "Increase release percentage to 50% during weekends when demand is higher."

This way, you maintain control over your resources while still contributing to the ecosystem.

---

### 3. **Use Priority Allocation Rules**

Another effective method is to implement priority allocation rules. This means that even if you're releasing a percentage of your resources, you can ensure that your own tasks always get priority during peak hours.

For instance, you can configure your system to say:

- "During 9 AM to 5 PM, allocate 100% of resources to my tasks."
- "During 10 PM to 4 AM, release 30% of resources to others, but still prioritize my tasks if needed."

This ensures that your own usage is never compromised, even if the system is under load during off-peak times.

---

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Releasing too much during peak hours:** This can lead to performance issues and may even cause your own tasks to be delayed or fail.
- **Not monitoring usage:** It's important to regularly check how your resources are being used and adjust your release percentage accordingly.
- **Ignoring platform-specific rules:** Some platforms have their own policies about how resources can be released. Make sure you're familiar with them to avoid any issues.
