**How Suppliers Can Set Release % Without Disrupting Daytime Use**

In the world of digital asset management, especially for developers and service providers, managing supply and demand is a delicate balance. One of the most common challenges is setting the right release percentage (release %) for your assets or services without affecting your own ability to use them during the day. This is especially crucial for those who rely on their own infrastructure for daily operations. In this article, we’ll walk through practical steps to help you set your release % effectively, while ensuring your system remains fully functional during peak hours.

---

### 1. Understand Your Usage Patterns

The first step in setting your release % is to understand your own usage patterns. Are you using your assets during the day for development, testing, or customer service? If so, you need to ensure that a portion of your resources is always available for these tasks.

Start by tracking your usage over a week. Use tools like server monitoring or cloud provider dashboards to see when your resources are most heavily used. Once you have a clear picture, you can determine how much of your supply you can safely release without impacting your daily operations.

---

### 2. Set a Dynamic Release Schedule

Instead of using a static release % that remains the same throughout the day, consider setting a dynamic release schedule. This means adjusting the percentage based on the time of day or specific events.

For example, you could release 100% of your resources during off-peak hours (like late evening or early morning) and lower the release % during peak hours. This approach ensures that your system remains responsive during busy times while still allowing you to monetize your assets when demand is low.

Tools like ComputeGate or similar platforms can help automate this process, allowing you to set rules based on time, demand, or other conditions.

---

### 3. Use a “Reserve” Percentage

Another effective strategy is to set aside a reserve percentage of your resources for your own use. This means that a certain portion of your assets will never be released, ensuring that you always have access to the resources you need during the day.

For instance, if you have 100 units of a resource, you might reserve 20 units for your own use and release the remaining 80 units to others. This approach gives you full control over your system’s availability while still allowing you to earn income from your assets.

Reserving a portion of your supply is especially useful if you're using the same infrastructure for multiple purposes, such as development, testing, and customer service.

---

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

While setting your release % might seem straightforward, there are several common mistakes that can lead to issues. One of the most frequent errors is releasing too much of your supply without considering your own usage. This can lead to system slowdowns or even downtime during the day.

Another mistake is not monitoring your system’s performance after adjusting your release %. It’s important to track how your changes affect your system and make adjustments as needed. Relying on static settings without regular reviews can lead to inefficiencies and missed opportunities.

Lastly, some suppliers try to maximize their release % without considering the impact on their own operations. This can lead to poor user experiences and long-term dissatisfaction from customers who rely on your services.
