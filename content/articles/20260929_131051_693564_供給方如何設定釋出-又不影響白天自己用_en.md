As a developer, your company is likely to have more than one product. You might be the creator of an app that allows people to rent out their unused space and you’re also developing a platform where users can book these spaces. How do you ensure you don’t eat into your own resources? In this article, we’ll look at how to set up a percentage release without affecting your day-to-day operations.
Setting up a percentage release is essential for any business that wants to monetize its products and services. This allows the company to earn revenue from the usage of their platform while still ensuring there’s enough money in the bank to cover daily expenses. However, it can be challenging to find the right balance between releasing too much or not enough.
In this article, we’ll discuss some actionable steps you can take to set up a percentage release that won’t impact your day-to-day operations. We’ll also highlight common mistakes and provide tips on how to avoid them.
1. Calculate Your Costs
Before setting up a percentage release, it’s essential to understand the costs associated with running your business. This includes salaries, rent, utilities, marketing expenses, and any other recurring fees.
Once you have a clear understanding of these costs, you can determine what percentage of revenue should be released to cover them. For example, if your total monthly costs are $10,000 and you want to keep 50% for yourself, then you would release 50% of the remaining 50%, or 25%. This means that only 25% of each transaction will be released as revenue.
2. Set a Buffer
It’s important to set aside a buffer in case your business experiences unexpected expenses or slow growth. Having a buffer can help ensure that you have enough money to cover any unforeseen costs, such as equipment repairs or marketing campaigns.
The size of the buffer depends on your specific needs and goals. For example, if you’re just starting out, you might want to set aside 10-20% of your revenue for a buffer. As your business grows, you can gradually reduce this percentage.
3. Use a Revenue Sharing Model
A revenue sharing model is an excellent way to balance releasing a percentage release while still ensuring that you have enough money to cover daily expenses. This model allows you to share the revenue generated by your platform with other users or partners who use your services.
For example, if you’re developing a marketplace for renting out unused space, you could offer a 10% discount on rental fees to users who refer new customers to your platform. This encourages users to promote your business while also generating additional revenue.
4. Set Up Automatic Payments
Automatic payments can help streamline the process of releasing percentages and ensure that you don’t miss any deadlines. By setting up automatic payments, you can automate the release of revenue from each transaction.

5. Monitor and Adjust
It’s important to monitor your revenue and expenses regularly to ensure that you’re releasing the right percentage of revenue. If you find that your business is struggling financially, you may need to adjust the release percentage or increase your buffer.
Additionally, it’s essential to keep an eye on trends in your industry and make adjustments as needed. For example, if you notice a decrease in rental rates for unused space, you may want to consider adjusting your revenue sharing model to reflect this change.
Common Mistakes
One of the most common mistakes businesses make when setting up a percentage release is releasing too much or not enough. Here are some tips on how to avoid these mistakes:
- Set realistic expectations: Determine what percentage of revenue you can realistically release without impacting your day-to-day operations.
- Be transparent with users: Clearly communicate your revenue sharing model and the impact it will have on their payments.
- Monitor trends: Keep an eye on industry trends and make adjustments as needed.
- Have a buffer: Set aside a buffer to cover unexpected expenses or slow growth.
Conclusion
Setting up a percentage release is essential for any business that wants to monetize its products and services. By following the actionable steps outlined in this article, you can ensure that your revenue sharing model doesn’t impact your day-to-day operations. Remember to calculate costs, set a buffer, use a revenue sharing model, set up automatic payments, and monitor trends regularly.
