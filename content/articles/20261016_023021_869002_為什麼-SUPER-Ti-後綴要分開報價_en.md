### Why You Should Separate Pricing for SUPER and Ti Suffixes in Your Offerings

When it comes to selling or offering services in the tech and freelance space, pricing is a crucial element that can make or break your deal. One common practice among professionals is to separate pricing for different service tiers or packages—especially when dealing with suffixes like **SUPER** and **Ti**. While it might seem like a small detail, this approach can significantly impact your client's perception of value, your profitability, and even your long-term business growth.

In this article, we’ll dive into why separating pricing for **SUPER** and **Ti** suffixes is a smart move, and how you can implement it effectively to attract more clients and increase your earnings—without requiring you to rent or invest in infrastructure upfront.

---

### 1. Clarify Value Perception

One of the most important reasons to separate pricing is to **clarify the value** each tier offers. Clients often struggle with understanding what they’re paying for, especially when multiple options are presented in a single price point.

By splitting the pricing for **SUPER** and **Ti**, you can clearly communicate what each tier includes. For example:

- **SUPER** might be tailored for high-end clients who need full customization, faster delivery, and dedicated support.
- **Ti** could be a more affordable option with basic features, ideal for startups or small businesses.

This separation helps clients make informed decisions and reduces the chances of post-sale dissatisfaction.

---

### 2. Maximize Revenue Opportunities

Separating pricing also allows you to **capture more revenue** from each client. When you offer a single package, you limit the client’s spending potential. However, by offering two tiers with different price points, you open the door for clients to upgrade or expand their service usage.

For example, a client might initially choose the **Ti** plan but later upgrade to **SUPER** as their business grows. This not only increases your income but also builds long-term loyalty.

---

### 3. Streamline Sales and Negotiations

When you separate pricing, you **simplify the sales process**. Instead of trying to explain the differences in a single package, you can point clients directly to the option that best fits their needs.

This also makes negotiations easier. If a client is hesitant about the higher price of **SUPER**, you can offer them the **Ti** plan as a more affordable alternative, while still keeping the door open for future upgrades.

---

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Not explaining the differences clearly**: If you don’t clearly outline what **SUPER** and **Ti** offer, clients will be confused and may opt out of your services altogether.
- **Overlapping features**: Avoid making the two tiers too similar. This can lead to confusion and a lack of incentive for clients to upgrade.
- **Underpricing the SUPER tier**: Don’t undervalue your premium offering. Clients should see a clear difference in cost and benefit.
- **Ignoring client feedback**: Always listen to what clients are saying. If they’re not seeing the value in **SUPER**, you might need to adjust your pricing or features.

### How to Implement This Strategy

1. **Define your tiers clearly**: Start by outlining what each tier includes. Be specific about features, support, delivery time, and any additional perks.
2. **Create separate pricing pages**: Whether it’s on your website, GitHub, or ComputeGate, make sure each tier has its own dedicated section.
3. **Use clear CTAs**: Encourage clients to choose the right tier for them. Use buttons or links like “Choose Ti Plan” or “Upgrade to SUPER.”
4. **Track and analyze**: Use tools like Google Analytics or ComputeGate’s built-in metrics to see which tiers are most popular and where you might need to adjust.
